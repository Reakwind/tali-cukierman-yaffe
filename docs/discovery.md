## 1. Purpose and organizing principle

Create one coherent professional hub for Tali's work across organizations.
Organize primarily by subject and visitor task, not by institutional structure.
Show the relevant organization and Tali's specific role beside each activity.

Keep academic work, clinical services, and commercial ventures distinguishable.
An affiliation is not an endorsement, and a coauthored publication does not
establish company ownership, product approval, or a current commercial role.

## 3. Initial personas and journeys

These are provisional, task-based personas, not invented interview participants.
Avoid demographic assumptions that do not affect the task. Student and academic
needs are informed by published NN/g research. The investor persona also draws
on NIH SEED guidance, but its relevance depends on the intended funding model.

### Prospective graduate student

- Goal: decide whether the research, supervision, and opportunity are a good fit.
- Questions: What does the group study? What methods will I learn? Who would
  supervise me? Are there current openings, eligibility rules, and funding?
- Friction: a long CV, unexplained acronyms, stale openings, or no application
  instructions. Do not claim that recruitment or funding is available without
  confirmation.
- Journey: home or a research landing page -> research theme/project -> people
  and opportunities -> explicit application/contact instructions.
- Content: short project summaries, representative papers, approved team
  profiles, mentorship information, and dated opportunity status.
- Prototype task: find one suitable project and explain the next application
  step without assistance.

### Investor or strategic funding partner

- Goal: assess relevance, evidence, maturity, and who to contact.
- Questions: What problem is being addressed? What evidence exists? Is this an
  academic project or a company activity? What has been validated, and what is
  the next milestone? Which organization owns or manages the work?
- Friction: hype, unsupported clinical claims, missing project status, or a
  venture mixed indistinguishably with hospital/university work.
- Journey: home or a project link -> innovation/project overview -> evidence
  and development stage -> partnership contact at the correct organization.
- Content: approved problem/evidence/milestone summaries, collaborators, dated
  status, and clear limitations. Publish IP, regulatory, financial, or fundraising
  details only with explicit approval; do not place confidential decks in a
  public repository.
- Prototype task: identify the project's evidence stage and the correct contact.
- Open question: does "investor" mean venture investment, philanthropy, grant
  funding, industry partnership, or several distinct audiences?

### Academic colleague or prospective collaborator

- Goal: assess scientific overlap and initiate a specific collaboration.
- Questions: What are the methods, populations, and current projects? Which
  publications are relevant? What was Tali's contribution? Who else is involved?
- Friction: marketing language, an unstructured publication wall, missing DOIs,
  or vague contact routes.
- Journey: search result/publication -> related research project -> methods,
  collaborators, and selected papers -> collaboration contact.
- Content: concise scientific summaries with optional detail, verified citations,
  DOI/PubMed links, and a clear contact route. Link to data/code only when an
  approved public resource exists.
- Prototype task: locate a relevant paper and associated project/contact from a
  deep link, without having to start at the homepage.

### Secondary audience to confirm

Patients and caregivers may arrive because of Tali's clinical roles. If included,
give them an obvious route to official Sheba services. Keep clinical enquiries
separate from research and commercial contacts. Do not collect medical histories,
appointment requests, or other sensitive health information in a generic form.

## 4. Proposed information architecture

This structure depends on the amount of approved content; merge thin sections
rather than launching empty pages.

- Home: name, concise purpose, principal work areas, affiliations, selected
  evidence, and clear routes to research, opportunities, and partnerships.
- About: biography, current roles by organization, clinical work, and approved CV.
- Research: themes and project pages connecting collaborators and publications.
- Innovation & partnerships: distinct academic/commercial activities, evidence,
  stage, and appropriate contact. Can begin as part of Research if content is thin.
- Publications: curated highlights and a verified list or institutional-profile
  link. Add client-side filtering only when the list warrants it.
- People & opportunities: real approved profiles, supervision, and dated openings.
  If there is no group roster, start with opportunities rather than invent a lab.
- Contact: separate research, student, partnership, and official clinical routes.

Use one consistent header/footer, a visible current-page state, descriptive link
labels, and breadcrumbs on nested pages. Use audience shortcuts as optional
entry points, not a forced "choose your persona" navigation gate. Every detail
page should offer a meaningful next step and a route back to its parent.

## 5. Design direction and reusable materials

Proposed direction: a warm, editorial academic website, not a hospital portal or
a generic venture-capital landing page. Off-white, dark ink, and one restrained
teal/green accent are a starting proposal, not approved brand decisions.

- IBM Carbon: use its grid, spacing, typography, and accessibility guidance.
  Its code is Apache-2.0 licensed. Reuse selectively with required notices;
  installing the whole component suite is unnecessary.
- USWDS: use clear navigation, readable content structure, and accessible
  interaction patterns. Check the license of each reused asset; third-party
  assets have their own terms. Do not copy government banners or seals.
- IBM Plex: candidate open-source Sans/Serif typography under SIL OFL 1.1.
  Self-host only needed font files/weights and retain the license. Confirm
  Hebrew coverage and a suitable pairing if the site is bilingual.

Visual rules:

- Use a small 4/8-based spacing scale, consistent alignment, and more space
  between unrelated groups than within a group.
- Start with roughly 16-24 px mobile gutters and generous, responsive section
  spacing; adjust against real content rather than locking every device to
  arbitrary breakpoints.
- Body copy around 18 px with comfortable line height and approximately 60-70
  characters per line. Use a restrained heading/weight scale, not many competing
  type styles.
- Put the subject and useful information at the start of headings and links.
  Summaries precede detail. Support scanning without assuming a universal eye
  movement pattern or treating every section as a card.
- Prefer whitespace, alignment, and subtle borders for grouping. Reserve shadows
  for actual elevation, such as an open menu.
- Use authentic, permission-cleared photography when available. No generated
  portraits, fabricated lab scenes, or invented scientific graphics.
- Aim for WCAG 2.2 AA, visible keyboard focus, reduced-motion support, usable zoom,
  and comfortably sized touch controls (aim for 44 px).
- Preserve access to content/navigation without client-side JavaScript. Avoid
  carousels, scroll hijacking, autoplay, hover-only menus, and decorative animation.

## 6. Recommended stack

Recommendation: Astro static generation, TypeScript, semantic HTML, and plain CSS.
Use native browser features and Astro's built-in capabilities before adding a
package or writing a custom equivalent. Add small progressive enhancements only
for demonstrated needs. No React, full UI framework, database, login, server-side
rendering, or custom API is justified by the current requirements.

Store content in Markdown/JSON with Astro content collections and validation.
Projects and publications should reference shared affiliation records rather
than duplicate role text. Keep the initial schema modest. An editing interface
is a separate decision: GitHub/agent-assisted PR edits are simplest; if editors
need visual editing, evaluate a maintained Git-backed CMS rather than building one.

Deploy the generated `dist/` directory to Cloudflare's free static hosting. Pages
offers straightforward Git integration and branch previews; Workers Static Assets
is another supported destination. Neither requires an Astro server adapter for a
fully static build. Confirm the deployment choice before creating cloud resources.

Budget constraints:

- No new paid subscriptions or metered AI automation by default.
- Standard GitHub-hosted runner compute is free for public repositories. Artifact
  and cache storage have separate allowances; retain only small, short-lived
  failure reports and avoid unnecessary artifacts.
- Pages Free currently allows 500 builds/month and 20,000 files. Preview builds
  count toward build usage. Workers static asset requests are free and unlimited;
  Worker-script execution is subject to separate quotas/pricing.
- Keep billing controls within the user's account authorization. A custom domain
  is optional and registration/renewal is not free hosting.
- A GitHub/Copilot subscription does not imply unlimited AI-agent usage. Confirm
  the exact subscription before enabling any hosted agent or paid review service.

## 7. Testing and definition of ready

Use red -> green -> refactor for behavior changes. Write an acceptance test first
and observe it fail for the intended missing behavior, then implement the smallest
change, verify it passes, and refactor with the suite green. Record the red/green
commands and results in the PR; unrelated setup failures are not meaningful reds.
Do not merge an intentionally failing test commit.

Proposed testing layers:

1. Static checks: Astro/TypeScript checks, ESLint, and Prettier (with Astro support).
2. Content/build checks: schemas, required metadata, unique slugs/identifiers,
   valid cross-references, and a successful static production build.
3. Unit tests: use Node's built-in runner for any real pure logic. Do not create
   artificial helper layers or add a second test framework merely for coverage.
4. Playwright: test the built site on Chromium, Firefox, and WebKit; include mobile
   viewports and the three primary visitor journeys. Check deep links, navigation,
   current-page state, contact routes, keyboard behavior, 404s, and internal links.
5. Accessibility: use maintained `@axe-core/playwright` integration plus manual
   keyboard, zoom, and screen-reader checks. Automated scans are not certification.
6. Responsive and resilience checks: narrow/wide screens, horizontal overflow,
   long titles, reduced motion, unavailable optional imagery, and JavaScript off.
   Add RTL/mixed-language cases if bilingual scope is approved.
7. SEO/production checks: titles, descriptions, canonical URLs once the domain is
   known, sitemap, social metadata, robots rules, and security response headers.
   Verify headers against deployed Cloudflare responses, not only local preview.
8. Performance: agree asset/JavaScript budgets; aim for good Core Web Vitals
   (LCP <= 2.5 s, INP <= 200 ms, CLS <= 0.1). Lab audits are useful regression
   signals, not a substitute for real-user field measurements.
9. External references: check periodically, separately from deterministic required
   PR checks so temporary third-party failures do not randomly block work.
10. Release checks: inspect the preview visually and smoke-test on an actual iPhone
    and Android device when available. Browser emulation is not physical-device
    testing; report unavailable manual checks honestly.

All feature-relevant automated checks must pass for the exact PR revision before
merge. Document manual results and remaining limitations. Start with modest
coverage targets for real logic rather than chasing 100% of static markup.

## 8. Repository security and agent workflow

These are planned controls, not settings already applied.

- Public repository under the confirmed owner and name. Confirm the code license
  separately from rights to biography text, photographs, logos, and publications.
- Seed only the minimum repository bootstrap on `main`. Every feature after that,
  including infrastructure, receives its own focused branch and PR.
- Protect `main`: require a PR, named CI checks, resolved conversations, current
  branch checks, and linear history; prohibit force pushes/deletion and apply
  protections to administrators. Allow squash merges only.
- Support the user's solo-agent workflow without requiring approval from the
  same account that authored a PR: GitHub does not allow self-approval. Require
  independent human approval only if a separate reviewer is available/agreed.
- The working agent inspects the diff and preview, verifies checks, writes a
  concise PR explanation (why/change/test evidence/limitations), then squash-merges
  and removes the feature branch when ready. Never bypass failing protections.
- An active agent can perform this workflow with the user's existing authorized
  tools. Repository settings alone do not create a continuously running AI worker.
- Add a concise `AGENTS.md`, setup/content-editing README, contribution guide,
  security reporting instructions, and issue/PR templates. Include exact local
  verification commands, Context7 use, content provenance, budget constraints,
  and the definition of ready.
- Enable supported free Dependabot alerts/updates, secret scanning/push protection,
  and appropriate public-repository code scanning. Review dependency upgrades;
  do not blindly auto-merge major changes.
- Lock dependencies, use reproducible installs, pin third-party Actions to commit
  SHAs, and set minimal workflow permissions. Run untrusted PR code without secrets;
  avoid privileged `pull_request_target` checkouts and public self-hosted runners.
- Do not put PATs, API keys, patient/student data, confidential research, or investor
  decks in commits. Grant Cloudflare's GitHub App access only to this repository.
- Add CSP and other appropriate static response headers; self-host assets and avoid
  third-party embeds/tracking by default. Verify deployment behavior before claiming
  protections are active. Do not enable HSTS preload without deliberate domain review.
- Require explicit authorization for billing, DNS changes, sensitive content, or
  permissions broader than this project. Never disable safeguards to unblock a PR.

Reference: https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/configuring-branches-and-merges-in-your-repository/managing-protected-branches/about-protected-branches

Suggested PR sequence:

1. Repository/CI/security foundation with failing acceptance checks before site work.
2. Accessible responsive design system, shared layout, and navigation.
3. Approved biography/affiliations, research, and publications content.
4. Student/opportunity and collaboration/innovation journeys.
5. Cloudflare previews/production configuration and release verification.

Split further when a PR would contain unrelated features. Do not turn this into a
large multi-service platform or add features without a demonstrated audience need.
