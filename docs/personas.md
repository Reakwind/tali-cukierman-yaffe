# Two working personas for content and journeys

Drafted 2026-10-31. These are design hypotheses built from the confirmed audience
priorities and public reference sites (NIHR "Be Part of Research", Ranade Lab,
ClinicalTrials.gov patient guidance), not interview findings. Validate with real
readers before treating them as evidence.

## Persona A — Dr. Maya, referring clinician / potential collaborator

- **Who:** hospital-based diabetologist or diabetes researcher (the site also
  serves academic collaborators and students — Maya covers that cluster).
- **Goal:** decide within minutes whether any study or research line is relevant
  to her patients or her own work, and find the right next step.
- **Reads like this:** scans headings and status first, distrusts marketing tone,
  expects precise but plain scientific wording; opens the official record to
  verify; will not forward a page that looks stale or promotional.
- **Needs from every study page:** what is being asked, for whom, at which sites,
  current status _with a date_, time/visit burden, and an honest route for
  referrals — plus research themes, methods, and publications for collaboration.
- **Friction to avoid:** registry jargon ("Registry lists recruiting",
  "Source checked"), undated status, buried official links, patient-style copy on
  pages she needs to share professionally.

## Persona B — Rivka, patient / family caregiver

- **Who:** an older adult with diabetes, or an adult child helping a parent
  consider research. May be reading on a phone, possibly stressed for time.
- **Goal:** understand whether a study _might_ be relevant and what taking part
  would actually involve — without medical jargon, without committing to anything.
- **Reads like this:** reads top-down, needs short sentences; "recruiting" must
  carry a date so she knows it is current; tests and visits need to appear early;
  she needs to be told that asking a question is not enrolling and that the study
  team decides eligibility.
- **Needs from every study page:** who it is for (in plain words), what happens
  and where, time commitment, optional vs required procedures, what happens when
  she expresses interest, and reassurance about not sending medical details.
- **Friction to avoid:** phrases written for registry auditors ("Who the registry
  describes"), implied promises of benefit, dead-end pages with no next step,
  fear that clicking "ask" means joining a trial.

## Shared journey rules these personas imply

1. Status words are plain ("Recruiting", "Completed") and always paired with a
   dated source note; the detail lives one line below, not inside the label.
2. Every study page answers, in order: what is this → is it for someone like me →
   what would it involve → what happens next → where to verify.
3. "Ask about taking part" language stays a question, never an application.
4. Clinician routes speak to professionals (referral, source records); participant
   routes speak to people (taking part, asking questions). Same facts, two tones.
5. No source bookkeeping in headings or labels — provenance lives in a quiet
   "Sources" section and dated update lines.
